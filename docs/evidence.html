The measurement

The same book, priced twice

A tokenized stock trades around the clock. The market that prices the share underneath it does not. When New York is shut, a market maker cannot hedge, so the quote widens - and the holder who needs out pays for it. Median spread across every quoted name: 267 bp while the US market is shut, 9 bp while it is open.

Spread by market phase

Every row is a median across every quoted symbol of that type in every snapshot taken during that phase. Phase is decided from the venue timestamp, not from the clock on the machine that ran the crawl.

Why the control column matters. The crypto pairs trade on the same venue, through the same matching engine, under the same fee schedule. Their liquidity has no reason to care whether the NYSE is open. If both columns moved together the effect would be venue-wide and this whole site would be wrong.

The worst hour to leave is not the middle of the night

Splitting the closed window into pre-market, after-hours and the genuinely shut hours was expected to flatter the overnight figure: the reference venue quotes in pre and post, thinly, so a maker can still hedge there. It did the opposite. Established names sit at 6.1 bp while New York trades, 55.5 bp in the shut hours, 65.1 bp after the close - and 92.9 bp in the pre-market, the widest of the four. The hour before the opening bell is the most expensive time to leave a tokenized stock, and it is the hour a holder reacting to overnight news is most likely to be trying to.

Why the split, and what it found

Splitting by how long a name has been listed was not cosmetic. Overnight, names listed thirty days or more sit at 56 bp; names listed more recently sit at 641 bp - 12x wider. A median over every listed name blends the two into 267 bp, which is why it moves when Bitget lists in bulk rather than when liquidity changes. Listing age predicts overnight spread better than anything else measured here, and a new listing is exactly the position a holder is least likely to know is expensive to leave.

This is a record in progress, not a finished study. The snapshot count behind each row is printed above so you can see how thin it still is. Nothing here is a claim about the future.

What a row does not say

A median spread is not a cost. It is the price of the first share, not of the position. What the position costs depends on size, and that is the method page. Whether the quote behind the spread is real at all is the validation page.

What is not validated yet

We cannot validate the liquid names, and that is the honest state of it. The validation page excludes 6 symbols — among them NVDA, TSLA, AAPL and MSFT — because two of the venue's own volume feeds disagree about them: the candle volume runs 1.4× to 774727.5× the 24h turnover the ticker reports for the same symbol. Which feed is right is not something this project can settle from outside, and scoring a prediction against a number that may be wrong would be worse than not scoring it. So the overnight widening holds as a quote, across every name the crawl can see. It is not yet established as a fill on the names most people actually hold. Those are the symbols where it matters most, and they are the ones still outstanding.

The same book, read twice

Every figure on this site comes through one HTTP client, and a bug in that client would look exactly like a property of the market. Since the argument here is that the venue's own numbers do not always agree with each other, the same scepticism is owed to our own reads. So the same exit is priced a second time through the Bitget Agent Hub CLI — a different SDK, in a different process, with no credentials — and both are costed by the same code, so any gap is the book and not the arithmetic.

What agreement does and does not prove. It does not prove the cost is right; both clients read the same exchange and would inherit the same error. It does rule out our transport as the explanation, which was previously unchecked. The two reads are sequential and seconds apart on a live book, so a sub-bp gap is the market moving between them rather than a disagreement — which is why the threshold exists and is printed rather than hidden. The failures are the interesting rows: where the Hub cannot return a two-sided book at all, that is a second client independently finding the same thinness that got those names excluded from validation.

Egress

Exit liquidity for tokenized US equities. Egress reads every listed instrument on Bitget every five minutes and measures what it costs to leave a position, at a stated size, from the book that exists.
